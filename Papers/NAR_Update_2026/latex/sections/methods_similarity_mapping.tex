\subsection{Reaction similarity and atom mapping}\label{sec:methods-mapping}

Reactions are compared by a structure-aware similarity operating on compound
structures rather than identifiers, so the same chemistry expressed in two
namespaces is recognised as one reaction.

Atom mappings are generated with the Reaction Decoder Tool and filtered:
retained only where every raw row is a canonical single-pair same-element
correspondence, with reactions requiring salvage tagged rather than discarded,
because a reaction needing one repair is likely to carry others. Mappings use
set notation on chemically indistinguishable atoms --- the two oxygens of
CO$_2$, the six equivalent terminal oxygens of pyrophosphate --- from each
compound's InChI-canonical equivalence classes, so the recorded ambiguity is
honest rather than false precision.
