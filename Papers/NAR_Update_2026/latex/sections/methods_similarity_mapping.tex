\subsection{Reaction similarity and atom mapping}\label{sec:methods-mapping}

Reactions are compared by a structure-aware similarity that operates on
compound structures rather than identifiers, so that the same chemistry
expressed in two namespaces is recognised as one reaction. The resulting
equivalence classes underpin the connectivity ontology reported below.

Atom mappings are generated with the Reaction Decoder Tool and filtered:
mappings are retained only where every raw row is a canonical single-pair
same-element correspondence, and reactions requiring salvage are tagged rather
than discarded, because a reaction with one repaired row is likely to carry
others that are subtly wrong. Mappings use set notation on atoms that are
chemically indistinguishable --- the two oxygens of CO$_2$, the six equivalent
terminal oxygens of pyrophosphate --- taken from each compound's
InChI-canonical equivalence classes, so the recorded ambiguity is honest rather
than a false precision. Supplementary Methods~S4 gives the filter and the
equivalence-class derivation.
