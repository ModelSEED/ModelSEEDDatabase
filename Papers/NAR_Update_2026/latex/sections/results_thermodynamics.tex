\subsection{Thermodynamics}\label{sec:results-thermo}

All three predictors now cover roughly half the database and disagree
substantially about how well they do it (Figure~\ref{fig:thermo}B). Group
contribution stores a value for essentially every reaction, but 26,555 of those
are a no-estimate placeholder; excluding them it covers 29,447 reactions,
against dGPredictor's 29,617 and eQuilibrator's 25,070. Coverage counts drawn
from raw entry counts overstate group contribution by a factor of two.

The three report uncertainty on scales that differ by more than an order of
magnitude (Figure~\ref{fig:thermo}C): median 0.6\,kcal\,mol$^{-1}$ for
eQuilibrator against 10.4 for group contribution and 17.0 for dGPredictor.
These are not comparable quantities, and a consumer choosing a source by
smallest reported error would systematically choose eQuilibrator, which is
precisely the source that understates. eQuilibrator additionally reports no
usable uncertainty at all for 3,730 reactions.

Protonation provenance is reported on two denominators
(Figure~\ref{fig:thermo}A) because one alone misleads. By compound the layer is
75\% open-source; by reaction, 87\% touch at least one compound whose ladder is
ChemAxon-derived. The minority are the polyprotic metabolites --- ATP, NAD(P),
phosphate, coenzyme~A --- that appear nearly everywhere.

\paragraph{A caveat on the rebuild.} Regenerating the protonation layer changed
133 reactions by more than 10\,kcal\,mol$^{-1}$. These are released with a
low-confidence flag and enumerated in Supplementary Table~S2. They are large
glycans whose reaction partners could not be re-protonated alongside them, so a
spurious ionizable site on one side is no longer cancelled by the same site on
the other. Neither the flagged value nor the previous release's value can be
checked against measurement: none of the 133 has an experimental match, and the
previous agreement arose from two errors cancelling rather than from accuracy.
