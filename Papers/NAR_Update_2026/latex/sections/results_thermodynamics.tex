\subsection{Thermodynamics}\label{sec:results-thermo}

All three predictors now cover roughly half the database and disagree
substantially about how well they do it (Figure~\ref{fig:thermo}B). Group
contribution stores a value for essentially every reaction, but 26,555 of those
are a no-estimate placeholder; excluding them it covers 29,447 reactions,
against dGPredictor's 29,617 and eQuilibrator's 25,070. Coverage counts drawn
from raw entry counts overstate group contribution by a factor of two.

Uncertainty is reported on scales differing by more than an order of magnitude
(Figure~\ref{fig:thermo}C): median 0.6\,kcal\,mol$^{-1}$ for eQuilibrator
against 10.4 and 17.0. A consumer choosing by smallest reported error would
therefore systematically choose the source that understates it. eQuilibrator
reports no usable uncertainty at all for 3,730 reactions.

Protonation provenance is reported on two denominators
(Figure~\ref{fig:thermo}A) because one alone misleads. By compound the layer is
75\% open-source; by reaction, 87\% touch at least one compound whose ladder is
ChemAxon-derived. The minority are the polyprotic metabolites --- ATP, NAD(P),
phosphate, coenzyme~A --- that appear nearly everywhere.

\paragraph{A caveat on the rebuild.} Regenerating the protonation layer moved
133 reactions by more than 10\,kcal\,mol$^{-1}$ --- large glycans whose partners
could not be re-protonated alongside them, so a spurious site on one side is no
longer cancelled by the same site on the other. They ship with a low-confidence
flag (Supplementary Table~S2). Neither the flagged value nor the previous
release's can be checked against measurement: none has an experimental match,
and the previous agreement came from two errors cancelling.
