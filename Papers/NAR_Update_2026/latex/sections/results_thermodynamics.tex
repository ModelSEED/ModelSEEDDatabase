\paragraph{Sensitivity to the protonation model.} Because the choice of pKa
model is invisible in the published output, we quantified how far it moves the
result. Recomputing the database under two protonation models and comparing
reactions scored by both, the effect concentrates sharply in the near-neutral
window: reactions containing a compound whose pKa differs between models in the
pH 6--8 range move by a median of 0.33\,kcal\,mol$^{-1}$, against
0.06\,kcal\,mol$^{-1}$ for reactions containing no affected compound --- roughly
an order of magnitude. The mechanism is that a pKa far from the reported pH
leaves the microspecies distribution saturated, whereas one near pH 7 decides
which species dominates.

The distribution is not, however, a smooth tail. \TBD[final counts against the
released tables] Of the reactions in the priority scope used for template
construction, \TBD[N] change assigned direction under the reversibility rule,
and these arise by two distinct mechanisms: a minority carry large energy shifts
on polyanionic substrates, while the remainder sit near a decision boundary and
are moved across it by a sub-kcal change. The second class is invisible to any
magnitude-based filter, which is an argument for reporting the ledger of
per-source directions described in Section~\ref{sec:methods-thermo} rather than
a single assignment.

On the compounds where the two models can be checked against measured
macroscopic ladders, the proprietary predictor reproduces the literature ladder
in \TBD[x] of \TBD[n] cases against \TBD[y] for the open-source predictor, the
gap arising entirely from polyprotic acids with chemically equivalent ionizable
sites. This asymmetry, rather than any overall accuracy difference, is what
determines the source order given in Section~\ref{sec:methods-thermo}.
