\subsection{Multi-source thermodynamics}\label{sec:methods-thermo}

The 2020 release combined two thermodynamics sources: eQuilibrator as the
primary estimator of \dfG{} and \drG{}, and a group-contribution (GC) method as
a fallback for compounds and reactions eQuilibrator could not estimate. For this
update we integrate four sources, each providing an independent estimate
wherever its coverage permits.

\paragraph{eQuilibrator refresh.} eQuilibrator 3.0~\cite{beber2022} was run
against the 2026 compound set at pH 7.0, ionic strength 0.25\,M, pMg 3.0 and
temperature 298.15\,K, matching the 2020 conditions. Rather than querying the
distributed compound cache directly, we rebuilt it from ModelSEED-curated
structures, so that every estimate derives from a structure this database
curates. Coverage: \TBD[N compounds with $\Delta_{\mathrm{f}}G'$, N reactions
with accepted $\Delta_{\mathrm{r}}G'$].

\paragraph{Protonation model.} eQuilibrator's Legendre transform to
physiological conditions requires, for each compound, a \emph{macroscopic}
acid-dissociation ladder: an ordered list in which consecutive species differ by
exactly one proton. The number of ladder entries above the reported pH sets the
proton count of the major microspecies, so the composition of the ladder changes
the transformed energy directly, not merely its precision.

Because the upstream cache derives these ladders by invoking a proprietary tool
(ChemAxon \texttt{cxcalc}), we evaluated replacing them with an open-source
predictor, MolGpKa~\cite{pan2021}. That substitution is only partly viable, for
a structural rather than an accuracy reason. MolGpKa predicts once per ionizable
atom on the neutral molecule, yielding \emph{microscopic} per-site constants;
chemically equivalent sites therefore return identical values, and a polyprotic
acid loses its second and subsequent dissociations. Orthophosphate, for
instance, is returned as three copies of $\mathrm{p}K_{a1}$. Against measured
values the predictor is accurate where the ionizable sites are distinct
(mean absolute error \TBD[MAE] over \TBD[n] measurements), so the limitation is
specific and bounded rather than general.

We therefore resolve protonation per compound from an ordered set of sources:
macroscopic literature ladders where a complete one exists~\cite{alberty2003},
then digitized measured values~\cite{iupac_pka}, then the ladders carried in the
pinned eQuilibrator cache, then MolGpKa. The ChemAxon-derived tiers are
consulted \emph{only} where MolGpKa provably cannot answer --- detected
mechanically, as a repeated predicted value, which is the signature of two
chemically equivalent sites returning the same number. This holds for 6,664 of
32,201 compounds (21\%).

The resulting composition is 75.1\% MolGpKa, 4.8\% digitized measured values and
0.01\% literature ladders, against 17.1\% ChemAxon-derived and 2.9\%
unattributed. \TBD[final counts if the build is regenerated]

Two consequences follow and are stated rather than elided. First, the
protonation layer is not fully open-source: it is reproducible by citation, in
that the cache is a pinned public release, but it is not regenerable from
scratch without a licensed tool. Second --- and this is the more important
number --- the ChemAxon-derived minority are the polyprotic metabolites of
highest reaction traffic, so although they are 17\% of compounds they
participate in \textbf{86\% of scored reactions}. Confining the substitution to
compounds where the open-source predictor degenerates therefore \emph{increases}
ChemAxon's reach through the database rather than reducing it, because those are
precisely the compounds that appear everywhere. We report the composition on
both denominators for that reason; the per-compound figure alone would
materially misrepresent the dependency.

Separately, the digitized measured values~\cite{iupac_pka} are licensed
CC-BY-NC-4.0 and are consumed by the pipeline but not redistributed with this
database, so a reader reconciling the released tables against the deposited
values will find entries sourced from a file that is not shipped.

\paragraph{Reactions where the protonation model is unreliable.} Substituting a
compound's pKa source changes how many of its sites lie above the reported pH,
and each such site places a proton on the major microspecies. Where a reaction's
partners are substituted too the change cancels; where they are not, it does
not, and $\Delta_{\mathrm{r}}G'^{\circ}$ moves by $RT\ln(10)\times\mathrm{pH}
= 9.55$\,kcal\,mol$^{-1}$ per unbalanced proton. This affects 133 reactions,
which are released with a low-confidence flag and enumerated in the
supplementary data. They are large glycans --- lipopolysaccharide, peptidoglycan
and lipid-linked oligosaccharides --- whose partners are equally large and for
which no experimental measurement exists, so neither the flagged value nor the
previous release's value can be checked against data. We note explicitly that
the previous value is not a safe fallback: there both partners carried the same
spurious sites and the errors cancelled, which is not the same as being correct.

\paragraph{Uncertainty and what it does not cover.} eQuilibrator reports
uncertainty as the covariance of the component-contribution fit. That covariance
carries no term for the protonation model, so it does not bound the effect
described above. Recomputing the database under two different pKa models, 210
reactions move by more than 1\,kcal\,mol$^{-1}$ \emph{and} by more than their
own quoted uncertainty; 85 move by more than 10\,kcal\,mol$^{-1}$, of which 68
are not covered by the reported uncertainty of either model. The affected
reactions are large polyanions --- lipopolysaccharide core, peptidoglycan,
O-antigen --- where many ionizable groups compound. We report the covariance as
published and flag this omission rather than inflating the reported values,
since the two quantities are not commensurable.

\paragraph{Group Contribution rebuilt from MFAToolkit.} The 2020 fallback GC
method has been rebuilt from the MFAToolkit implementation against the current
compound set, so the group definitions and parameter fits reflect the enlarged
compound base rather than the 2020 snapshot. Coverage: \TBD{}.

\paragraph{dGpredictor retrained on ModelSEED.} dGpredictor (an ML-based
$\Delta G$ estimator) has been retrained using the ModelSEED-curated compound
structures as training data. This produces a ModelSEED-native estimator rather
than one trained on a competing biochemistry database, and lets us report
$\Delta G$ estimates for compounds the other three methods cannot cover.
Training procedure and hyperparameters: \TBD{}. Coverage: \TBD{}.

\paragraph{OpenTECR integration.} OpenTECR is a community effort to standardize
experimentally-measured thermodynamic constants; we integrate its values where a
mapping between OpenTECR reaction identifiers and ModelSEED reaction IDs exists.
Coverage: \TBD{}.

\paragraph{Direction assignment.} For each reaction with an accepted \drG{}
(from any source, chosen with per-compound-consistency constraints described in
the 2020 paper), we apply the same reversibility rules used in 2020 (based on
Jankowski \textit{et al.}~\cite{jankowski2008}) to assign one of \texttt{=}
(reversible), \texttt{\textgreater} (irreversible left-to-right), or
\texttt{\textless} (irreversible right-to-left). On top of this we add
per-reaction-class heuristic overlays --- hard rules for classes where the
standard thermodynamic assignment is known to disagree with textbook biology.
Rule set: \TBD[discussed in the working session but not yet codified in a repo
script]. Overlays are applied after the base assignment and their scope is
documented per class.

\paragraph{Combined direction ledger.} For every reaction, the pipeline records:
the \drG{} estimate from each of the four sources (with per-source uncertainty),
the base direction assignment from each source, the heuristic overlay if
applicable, and the final direction. This ledger is the input to the
reaction-direction sensitivity study reported in
Section~\ref{sec:results-direction}.
