\subsection{Multi-source thermodynamics}\label{sec:methods-thermo}

The 2020 release combined eQuilibrator with a group-contribution fallback. This
update ships four sources --- eQuilibrator~3.0~\cite{beber2022}, group
contribution rebuilt from MFAToolkit, dGPredictor~\cite{wang2021} retrained
on ModelSEED structures, and experimental values where they exist --- each
stored as its own \texttt{[energy, uncertainty, direction]} record so that no source is
overwritten by another. All values are reported at pH~7.0, ionic strength
0.25\,M, pMg~3.0 and 298.15\,K; Supplementary Methods~S3 explains why that
combination matches neither eQuilibrator preset.

\paragraph{Protonation.} eQuilibrator's transform requires a \emph{macroscopic}
pKa ladder per compound: an ordered list in which consecutive species differ by
one proton. The count of entries above the reported pH sets the proton count of
the major microspecies, so the composition of the ladder changes the
transformed energy directly, not merely its precision.

We resolve each compound from measured literature ladders where a complete one
exists~\cite{alberty2003, iupac_pka}, otherwise from the open-source predictor
MolGpKa~\cite{pan2021}. That predictor is accurate where a compound's ionizable
sites are chemically distinct, but it predicts once per atom on the neutral
molecule, so equivalent sites return the same value and a polyprotic acid loses
its second and subsequent dissociations --- orthophosphate is returned as three
copies of its first pKa. The failure is detectable as a repeated predicted
value, true of 6,664 of 32,201 compounds, and only there do we fall back to the
ChemAxon-derived ladders in the eQuilibrator cache.

Two consequences are stated rather than elided. The layer is not fully
open-source: it is reproducible by citation, the cache being a pinned public
release, but not regenerable without a licensed tool. And although the
ChemAxon-derived compounds are 20\% of the total, they are the most connected
polyprotic metabolites, so they participate in 87\% of scored reactions
(Figure~\ref{fig:thermo}A) --- restricting substitution to where the open
predictor provably fails \emph{increases} its reach rather than reducing it.

\paragraph{Uncertainty.} The sources report on scales that are not comparable
(Figure~\ref{fig:thermo}C): against 802 experimentally-anchored reactions,
group contribution overstates its error by roughly 2.2$\times$ and
eQuilibrator understates by 1.6$\times$. eQuilibrator's figure is the
component-contribution covariance and carries no protonation term at all, so it
does not bound the effect described above.

\paragraph{Direction.} Reversibility is assigned from the accepted energy by
the rule set of Jankowski \textit{et al.}~\cite{jankowski2008}, unchanged from
2020, with eQuilibrator as the primary source and group contribution as
fallback. Applying the 2020 rules to the 2026 energies leaves 25,060 reactions
with no direction call, having neither an eQuilibrator nor a
group-contribution energy.
