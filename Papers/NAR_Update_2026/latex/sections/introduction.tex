\section{Introduction}

Genome-scale metabolic models are only as good as the biochemistry beneath
them. The ModelSEED Biochemistry Database was built to supply that layer:
a mass- and charge-balanced reaction network, reconciled across the major
biochemical databases, with the structures, thermodynamics and atom mappings
that reconstruction and analysis require~\cite{seaver2020}.

Six years on it has grown by a third in compounds and half in reactions, and
the constraints have shifted: growth comes from sources supplying reactions
faster than structures, so curation rather than collation is now limiting, and
the thermodynamic layer carries four independent and frequently disagreeing
sources where it once carried one.

This update reports that growth, the structure-curation pipeline built to
recover balance where sources conflict, and a thermodynamic layer that
publishes every source with its own uncertainty rather than promoting one. We
argue that the disagreement between sources is itself the useful signal, and
grade every reaction's thermodynamic evidence against experimental
measurements so that a consumer can see which numbers carry weight.
