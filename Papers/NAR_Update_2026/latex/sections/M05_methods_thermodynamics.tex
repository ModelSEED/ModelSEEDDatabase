\subsection{Multi-source thermodynamics}\label{sec:methods-thermo}

The 2020 release combined two thermodynamics sources: eQuilibrator~\cite{flamholz2012} and the group contribution method~\cite{jankowski2008}.
This ModelSEED Database update incorporates the most recent eQuilibrator~3.0~\cite{beber2022} and introduces two new thermodynamics sources: dGPredictor~\cite{wang2021} and experimental values.
Each of the four distinct thermodynamics sources are reported separately, where available.
Only the experimental values carry a published direction.
The other three sources are energy estimates and their resulting directions are inference.
All values are reported at pH~7.0, ionic strength 0.25\,M, pMg~3.0 and 298.15\,K; the basis for each condition and its effect on the released energies are given in Supplementary Methods~S2.
Although the database is generalizable, transport reactions are scored from only stoichiometry and energy, thus ignoring membrane potentials and pH gradients.

\paragraph{Protonation.} eQuilibrator's Legendre transform requires a \emph{macroscopic} pKa ladder per compound: an ordered list in which consecutive species differ by one proton.
The count of entries around the desired pH sets the proton count of the major microspecies, so the composition of the ladder changes the transformed energy directly.
We generate the pKa ladders for every compound with a structure (source prevenance preserved), and the measured ladders of Alberty~\cite{alberty2003}, using licensed ChemAxon Marvin~\cite{marvin} for use with eQuilibrator and also via open-source MolGpKa~\cite{pan2021} for unlicensed use. 

\paragraph{Experimental anchors.} eQuilibrator and dGPredictor draw on the same set of experimental values derived from the NIST Thermodynamics of Enzyme-catalyzed Reactions (TECR) Database~\cite{goldberg2004}.
A challenge confronted here was structural conflicts in compounds mapped to ModelSEED from the KEGG IDs of TECR and the MetaNetX IDs of eQuilibrator.
To facilitate direct comparisons between thermodynamic predictions of ModelSEED reactions, we re-built the experimental anchors of TECR values and curated conflicts to directly map TECR to ModelSEED.
We further investigated openTECR, a communal re-curation of TECR available at \url{https://github.com/opentecr}, to extend the thermodynamic anchors.

\paragraph{Uncertainty.} The different thermodynamic sources estimate reaction free energy uncertainties differently, and consequently uncertainty is not directly comparable (Figure~\ref{fig:thermo}C)).

\paragraph{Direction.} To conserve precedence, we use the same 2020 heuristics to derive reaction directions from the Group contribution approach~\cite{jankowski2008}, so that series stays comparable across releases.
For eQuilibrator and dGPredictor, we use one rule set built on the reversibility index from Noor \textit{et al.}~\cite{noor2012}: a reaction is called irreversible when $|\ln\Gamma|$ exceeds $\ln(1000)$ by more than one propagated standard deviation~\cite{beber2022}, $\Gamma$ being the fold-change of reactant concentration required to change reaction direction).
However, we extend the rule to require that the uncertainty clears the threshold as well.
We report fewer directions than the published index would on the same energies, and we separate \emph{reversible} reactions from \emph{undetermined} cases except for reactions whose uncertainty is much smaller than the threshold yet the estimate lies within one standard deviation of the threshold, which we call reversible.
