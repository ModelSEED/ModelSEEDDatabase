cross-validation, refitting the calibration on each training fold and scoring
only the anchors held out of it, eQuilibrator's gold tier falls within
2\,kcal\,mol$^{-1}$ of measurement 92.5\% of the time ($n=352$). Its own
bronze tier holds too few anchors to support a rate; the separation is clearest
where the samples are large, group contribution scoring 77.2\% at silver
($n=452$) against 24.8\% at bronze ($n=335$).

Direction assignment inherits those grades (Figure~\ref{fig:direction}A). Gold
reactions are more often given a definite direction than bronze ones, and the
sources contribute asymmetrically (Figure~\ref{fig:direction}B): eQuilibrator
earns 73\% of gold assignments but only 3\% of bronze, while group contribution
supplies 74\% of silver and 78\% of bronze. A direction is therefore not a
uniform claim, and the grade is published alongside it.

Applying the 2020 rules to the 2026 energies leaves 25,060 reactions with no
call at all, having no energy from either primary source. That is the honest
ceiling on direction coverage from thermodynamics alone, and it is the argument
for the reaction-classification work reported separately.
