\section{Evidence grading}\label{sec:s3}

Every reaction carrying at least one thermodynamic estimate is graded gold, silver or bronze. The grade describes \emph{the reaction}, formed from the collective evidence available for it; the per-source machinery below exists to arrive at that single grade and is not itself published.

\subsection{The band} All tests are referred to $\tau = 2.0$\,kcal\,mol$^{-1}$, the half-width of the reversible band used by the direction cascade. A source is ``right'' on a reaction if it lands within $\tau$ of the reference value.

\subsection{Calibrated confidence} Reported uncertainties are not comparable across sources --- group contribution overstates its error by roughly $2.2\times$ and eQuilibrator understates by $1.6\times$ --- so raw $\sigma$ cannot be thresholded directly. For each source we instead fit \[ p_{\text{ok}} \;=\; P\!\left(\left|\Delta G_s - \Delta G^{*}\right| \le \tau \;\middle|\; \sigma_s\right) \] by isotonic regression, using the experimentally anchored reactions (weight 3) and, where no anchor exists, the deviation from a tight-uncertainty reference (weight 1). A probability is comparable across sources in a way $\sigma$ is not.

\subsection{Combining sources} To ask whether the sources agree, they are combined by precision on the \emph{calibrated} error scale $\hat{e}_s$ (floored at 0.3\,kcal\,mol$^{-1}$ so the weights stay finite), $w_s = 1/\hat{e}_s^{2}$: \[ \Delta G_{\text{fused}} = \frac{\sum_s w_s \Delta G_s}{\sum_s w_s}, \qquad \chi^{2} = \sum_s w_s \left(\Delta G_s - \Delta G_{\text{fused}}\right)^{2}, \qquad R = \sqrt{\frac{\chi^{2}}{n-1}} . \] $\Delta G_{\text{fused}}$ is an internal construct --- a reference point against which each source is measured --- and is never published or recommended.

$R$ is the Birge ratio, the scale factor the Particle Data Group applies to discrepant measurements of one quantity. Its interpretation is the whole point: if the reported uncertainties are honest, the scatter among sources should be about the size of those uncertainties, giving $R \approx 1$. Values well above 1 mean the sources lie further apart than their own error bars permit, so at least one is wrong. The residual $z_s = |\Delta G_s - \Delta G_{\text{fused}}| / \hat{e}_s$ then identifies which.

\subsection{The rules} Applied in this order, with measurement applied last so that it wins: \begin{enumerate} \item \textbf{Self-certainty.} $p_{\text{ok}} \ge 0.90$ grades gold, $\ge 0.70$ silver, otherwise bronze. \item \textbf{Corroboration} ($R \le 2.0$ and $z_s \le 2.0$) lifts bronze to silver, and no further. \item \textbf{Being outvoted} ($R > 2.0$ and $z_s > 2.0$) costs one tier. The same pair of constants governs both directions: a set is either concordant or it is not, and a separate, laxer bar for disagreement would have meant a source could be neither corroborated nor outvoted on grounds of arithmetic rather than evidence. \item \textbf{Measurement} against an experimentally anchored value overrides everything: within 1.0\,kcal\,mol$^{-1}$ is gold, 1.0--3.0 silver, beyond 3.0 bronze. It therefore demotes as readily as it promotes. \end{enumerate}

Reactions where every source reports $|\Delta G| < 0.5$\,kcal\,mol$^{-1}$, and all transport reactions, are flagged as structural zeros: their concordance is imposed by the stoichiometry rather than earned. An earlier version of the scheme excluded them from corroboration on the reasoning that agreement one could not avoid earns no credit. They are no longer excluded, because the premise did not survive testing: against the anchors they are the most accurate reactions in the set (near-zero 100\% within $\tau$, $n=132$; transport 95\%, $n=75$; everything else 79\%, $n=2{,}183$). Transport is flagged in the database independently, so a consumer who wants to treat these separately still can.

\subsection{Why agreement cannot confer gold} The treatment of cross-source behaviour is deliberately asymmetric. Agreement between two fallible predictors is weak evidence: eQuilibrator and group contribution share lineage, both descending from group contribution, so their concordance is partly one method agreeing with itself. Disagreement is strong evidence, because one of them is definitely wrong and $z_s$ names it.

The cost of the alternative was measured. Allowing corroboration to promote to gold grew eQuilibrator's gold column from 2,443 to 9,157 reactions while diluting its agreement with measurement from 94\% to 90\% within $\tau$ --- nearly four times the gold for a four-point loss in what gold means. Hence the cap.

\subsection{Grades and the routes into them}

Every grade is composed of two judgements: what the deciding source's own calibrated confidence claims, and what the other sources made of it. Both ship alongside the grade in the \texttt{thermo-evidence} block. The cross-source verdict is omitted where a check ran and resolved neither way --- the set is discrepant but this source is not the outlier, or the reverse.

Conflict is not the complement of corroboration. $R$ and $z$ test agreement on the \emph{energy}; direction compares $|\ln\Gamma|$ against $\ln(1000)$. Two sources can sit inside each other's error bars and still fall on opposite sides of that threshold, which is why 1{,}233 of the 3{,}863 gold-corroborated reactions carry a direction conflict.

\subsection{Validation} Grades are validated by five-fold cross-validation repeated four times, refitting the calibration on each training fold and scoring only the anchors held out of it, with the measurement override disabled so that a reaction cannot be graded and scored by the same number.
